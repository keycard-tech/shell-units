What is being checked

The Shell signs SHA256(uid ‖ challenge) with a key derived from its hardware secret. Two public keys satisfy any such signature, so both are recovered and hashed with the uid; a match against the file is the verification. The signature is the proof — the device's identity comes out of it, which is why the database never has to store or reveal a key.

Absence is reported separately from refusal: "not in this database" means a well-formed answer from something that is not on the list, while a refusal means the file, the message or the format is wrong and no conclusion about the device can be drawn.

Nothing leaves this page. The database is a file, the camera frames are never uploaded, and the challenge is generated here and thrown away after one attempt.
